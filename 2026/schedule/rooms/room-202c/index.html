Computable Contracts for Healthcare - Contracts-as-Code

Room 202C | 4 p.m. - 5 p.m.

What would an open-source machine-readable contract look like, aka, contracts-as-code? US healthcare has a ton of administrative waste, complexity, and non-deterministic elements that lead to surprises for both patients and B2B. There are payer-provider contracts and payer policies that could be made to more portable specifications; ie, JSON, a small DSL, or typed Python. Folks who have built computable contracts in other domains (tax, benefits, derivatives, energy) are super welcome and wanted. Topic ideas - who has encoded a contract or policy in code, in what domain, what broke - How well can AI extract from unstructured data to a machine-readable spec? - Representation choices: JSON Schema, a small DSL, Pydantic or dataclass models, decision tables - Rules engines: Drools (Java, business rules), Clara (Clojure, RETE), and Python equivalents - Portability: pyodide and the browser story, running the same spec server-side and client-side - Fuzzing a claims pricer: synthetic claims in, priced output out, the pair as a data contract - Cross-domain borrowing: what tax (Catala), benefits eligibility, and derivatives (FpML, CDM) figured out first - Local-first - Transparent SDKs What this is NOT about: smart contracts in the crypto sense

Financial Data with Python: APIs, Open Data, and Better Investment Workflows

Room 202C | 10 a.m. - 11 a.m.

Financial data powers investing, research, risk management, portfolio construction, and many other investment-related workflows, but it can also be expensive, fragmented, difficult to license, and hard to integrate cleanly into modern Python tools. This Open Space is for anyone interested in market data, fundamentals, ETFs, portfolio analytics, risk models, alternative data, financial APIs, or building investment-related tools with Python. We’ll discuss practical questions like: * When should you build your own financial data store versus relying on APIs or vendor platforms? * How do different financial data sources compare on cost, coverage, quality, licensing, and ease of use? * What do existing platforms such as Bloomberg, FactSet, MSCI Barra, LSEG Data & Analytics, and others do especially well, and where are there still opportunities for better developer experience, interoperability, transparency, or accessibility? * How can Python, open standards, better APIs, and community-driven tooling improve financial data workflows? * What would a modern, Python-native financial data platform look like? The goal is a collaborative discussion among developers, investors, data engineers, researchers, quants, students, and anyone curious about financial data. We’ll learn from what works today, identify common pain points, and brainstorm better tools, workflows, and possibilities for the next generation of financial data platforms.
